1st Amendment — Freedom of Speech & Religion

Main Focus Case: West Virginia Board of Education v. Barnette (1943)

Holding: Public schools cannot compel students to salute the flag or recite the Pledge of Allegiance. Forced patriotism violates First Amendment free speech and free exercise rights.


Petitioner (School Board): Argued compulsory flag salutes build national unity and patriotism during wartime.

Respondent (Barnette): Argued forced salutes violate religious beliefs against idolatry and compel speech contrary to conscience.

Additional 1st Amendment Cases

  • Hazelwood School District v. Kuhlmeier (1988): School administrators have the right to censor articles in school-sponsored student newspapers for legitimate educational concerns.
  • Brandenburg v. Ohio (1969): Free speech protects inflammatory political rhetoric unless it directly incites immediate lawless action.

2nd Amendment — Right to Bear Arms

Main Case: NYSRPA v. Bruen (2022)

Holding: Protects an individual's right to carry a firearm outside the home for self-defense.


Mentions:

  • US v. Cruikshank (1876): Early precedent holding the 2nd Amendment restricted only federal actions.
  • Presser v. Illinois (1886): States can prohibit private armed militias from parading in public.

3rd Amendment — Quartering Soldiers

Main Case: Mitchell v. Harmony (1852)

Holding: Military commanders cannot seize or occupy civilian property without immediate emergency necessity.


Mentions:

  • Laird v. Tatum (1972): Used 3rd Amendment principles to limit military surveillance of civilians.
  • US v. Russell (1871): Government must compensate owners if private property is taken for military use.

4th Amendment — Search & Seizure

Main Case: Kyllo v. United States (2001)

Holding: Police using thermal imaging to scan a private home from outside counts as an illegal search without a warrant.


Mentions:

  • Chimel v. California (1969): Warrantless search during arrest is strictly limited to suspect's immediate reach.
  • Arizona v. Gant (2009): Restricted police vehicle searches post-arrest to immediate safety threats or evidence.

5th Amendment — Self-Incrimination & Due Process

Main Case: Schmerber v. California (1966)

Holding: Mandatory blood samples for DUI tests do not violate the 5th Amendment because protection covers testimonial evidence, not physical samples.


Mentions:

  • Counselman v. Hitchcock (1892): Protected self-incrimination rights during grand jury proceedings.
  • US v. Hubbell (2000): Forcing massive document production can violate self-incrimination rights.

6th Amendment — Right to Fair Trial

Main Case: Batson v. Kentucky (1986)

Holding: Prosecutors cannot exclude potential jurors from a trial panel solely because of their race.


Mentions:

  • Taylor v. Louisiana (1975): Banned excluding women from jury pools to ensure a fair cross-section.
  • Pointer v. Texas (1965): Guaranteed defendants the right to confront witness testimony directly.

7th Amendment — Civil Jury Trials

Main Case: Dimick v. Schiedt (1935)

Holding: Federal judges cannot arbitrarily increase payout amounts awarded by civil juries.


Mentions:

  • Baltimore & Carolina Line v. Redman (1935): Balanced judicial rulings with civil jury responsibilities.
  • Colgrove v. Battin (1973): Established that 6-person juries satisfy 7th Amendment civil trial rights.

8th Amendment — Cruel & Unusual Punishment

Main Case: Atkins v. Virginia (2002)

Holding: Executing individuals with severe intellectual disabilities is illegal and counts as cruel and unusual punishment.


Mentions:

  • Miller v. Alabama (2012): Banned mandatory life sentences without parole for juvenile offenders.
  • Estelle v. Gamble (1976): Ignoring prisoner medical needs constitutes cruel and unusual punishment.
